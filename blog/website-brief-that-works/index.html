What a good website brief looks like

By the Renetter team4 min read

Most website projects that run late were late on day one, because the brief was vague. A good brief does not need to be long. It needs to answer a few questions plainly.

1. Who is the page for?

Name one primary visitor. A clinic owner and a first-time patient need different pages. If you try to speak to everyone, the page speaks to no one.

2. What should they do next?

Pick a single main action: book a call, request a quote, start a trial. Every section of the page should either lead to that action or remove a doubt that blocks it.

3. What do they doubt?

List the objections you hear on sales calls. Price, timelines, whether you serve their kind of business, whether you will still be around next year. A strong page answers these before the visitor has to ask.

4. What proof do you have?

Reviews, named customers, certifications, a short case study. Real proof beats clever copy. If you do not have it yet, say so, and we plan how to collect it.

5. What does success look like?

Be specific: more enquiries from search, fewer unqualified leads, a faster page on mobile. A clear goal lets us decide what to build first and what to leave for later.

  • Share two or three sites you like and say why.
  • Send the pages you already have, even if they are rough.
  • Tell us your deadline and what is driving it.
