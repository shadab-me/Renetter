Where AI automation pays off first

By the Renetter team5 min read

Most businesses do not need a grand AI strategy. They need one repetitive task taken off a person's plate, done reliably. Start there.

Look for work that is repeated, rule-based and boring

Good first candidates share three traits. They happen often, they follow a pattern a new hire could learn in a day, and a mistake is cheap to catch. Examples are sorting incoming enquiries, drafting first replies for a human to approve, summarising calls into a CRM, and answering the same support questions.

Keep a person in the loop at first

Let the system draft and a person approve. Once you trust the output on real cases, you can remove the approval step for the simplest categories and keep it for the rest.

Use your own documents

An assistant that answers from your own policies, product sheets and past tickets is far more useful than a generic chatbot. This is usually done with retrieval: the system looks up the right passage first, then writes the answer from it. It also makes answers easier to check.

Measure before and after

Time one week of the manual task first. After launch, measure the same thing. If the saving is small, stop. If it is large, move to the next process.

What to leave alone

  • Decisions with legal or safety consequences.
  • Anything where a wrong answer is expensive and hard to notice.
  • Processes that change every week and have no stable rules.
